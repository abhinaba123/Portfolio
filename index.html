01 / INTERNAL ENTERPRISE INITIATIVE

Driving Agile Delivery & Go-Live Readiness in a Changing Environment

Enterprise operations and RBAC access platform · 14-member cross-functional team · Agile delivery, UAT and release coordination

Business objective

Replace slow, manually coordinated employee access provisioning with a centralized role-based access control (RBAC) workflow. The goal was near-immediate access to supported enterprise resources; AI tools still required some manual intervention.

Results & impact

Helped drive the platform to production go-live, enabling near-immediate RBAC-based provisioning for supported resources instead of the previous days-long process. Established a structured three-phase business UAT and sign-off approach despite rotating delivery capacity. Separately, AI-enabled governance improvements reduced manual RAID reporting effort by 80% and weekly status report preparation effort by 75%.

Explore my role, delivery approach & decisions

Delivery challenge

Team members regularly rotated between projects, there was no permanently assigned QA team, and business stakeholders frequently revised requirements. At the same time, the organization wanted to go live quickly to realize access-provisioning benefits.

My responsibility

As Program Manager Associate, coordinated changing engineering and testing capacity, facilitated Scrum ceremonies, groomed and prioritized the backlog, structured business-user UAT and drove cross-team release readiness. Staffing and product decisions remained collaborative.

Agile execution & prioritization

  • Facilitated sprint planning, regular Scrum check-ins, sprint reviews and retrospectives to maintain visibility despite team turnover.
  • Led backlog grooming with stakeholders to clarify acceptance criteria and prioritize high-impact, go-live-critical work as requirements evolved.
  • Tracked owners, impediments, dependencies, delivery risks and scope changes to make trade-offs visible and keep handoffs moving.

UAT strategy & stakeholder alignment

  • Formulated a three-phase UAT plan to involve business users from relevant operational functions.
  • Coordinated test execution, issue follow-up, business feedback and phased sign-off to inform release decisions.
  • Aligned engineering, available QA support and business stakeholders around priority defects, readiness criteria and go-live activities.

Scrum metrics & delivery insights

Formulated and tracked six delivery and quality indicators: sprint velocity, sprint burndown, planned-versus-completed sprint work, defect trends, UAT pass rates and cycle time. Used the metrics in sprint reviews and delivery discussions to surface bottlenecks, assess progress and inform backlog prioritization as team capacity and requirements changed.

03 / EXTERNAL CLIENT · POC TO CUSTOMER PILOT

Mobile EV Charging Platform & Customer Dashboard

Three-week POC · Subsequent dashboard delivery · 7-person cross-functional team

From proof of concept to customer pilot

01POC3-week delivery
02ValidationClient acceptance
03DiscoveryFunctional requirements
04BuildUser stories & sprints
05PilotCustomer dashboard use

Business context & delivery challenge

A mobile EV charging service dispatched charging trucks to customer sites. The engagement began with a time-boxed proof of concept (POC). Following the client's acceptance of that POC, the scope evolved to a dashboard supporting charging operations for an initial customer pilot. The team had limited experience in parts of the domain, and delivery required rapid requirements clarification, frequent feedback cycles and close client communication. The existing operating process remained available as a fallback during the pilot.

Results & impact

Delivered the initial POC in three weeks through coordinated team execution. Following client acceptance, supported delivery of a dashboard that was used by the client's first customer during a pilot. Earned client appreciation for proactive communication and follow-through. Pilot usage is not represented as a full production-scale launch.

Explore my role, delivery approach & decisions

My role

As Project Manager, I coordinated team delivery, facilitated technical touchbase sessions between the client and engineers, gathered and clarified functional requirements, and broke approved needs into user stories for implementation. I managed follow-ups, priorities and stakeholder updates; engineering owned the technical design and development.

Approach & decisions

  • Phase 1 — POC: Coordinated sprint activities, priorities and dependencies to deliver the initial proof of concept within three weeks.
  • Phase 2 — Dashboard: After POC acceptance, facilitated client discovery sessions, translated dashboard requirements into actionable user stories and coordinated their implementation by the engineering team.
  • Team enablement: Helped onboard a senior software engineer to support a relatively inexperienced team and strengthen delivery capacity.
  • Agile delivery: Supported a strong sprint delivery cadence through planning, follow-ups and timely clarification of changing requirements; avoided treating velocity as a standalone success metric.
  • Client communication: Sent regular email updates on deployed features, reported defects and requested improvements, including estimated delivery or resolution dates; received positive feedback for consistent follow-up.

Delivery considerations

Distinguished POC validation from subsequent dashboard requirements and customer pilot use. Coordinated fixes and improvements while the client retained an established fallback process in case pilot workflows failed. Did not own technical implementation or claim a full commercial rollout.
